# The fifth step, not the smaller step

**Recommendation for LUV IT · plasmajo.com · 7 September 2026 · launch in 7 days**

Everything catalogue-related below I re-verified myself against the repo, not against the packs. Files named inline.

---

## 1. The load-bearing fact

**The brief's premise survives, but it is not the useful fact. The useful fact sits one level above it: the fastest-emptying product in the whole catalogue is not in the box at all for three of the five routines.**

I confirmed the compositions in `D:\Ryan-Work\LUVIT\luvit\_وارد\woo-products.json`:

| id | SKU | contents | price |
|---|---|---|---|
| 203 روتين الإشراقة | 4 parts | L111 + L114 + **L101 فيتامين سي** + L116 | 48.00 |
| 204 روتين التنقية والتوازن | 4 parts | L110 + **L105 (5% + 0.5% AHA+BHA)** + L101 + L116 | 48.00 |
| 205 روتين الترطيب والدعم | 4 parts | L111 + L114 + **L102 (twice daily)** + L116 | 47.00 |
| 367 / 527 روتين توحيد اللون | 5 parts | … + L119 + **L112 واقي الشمس** | 70.70 |

And a correction to the brief: **the sunscreen's size IS published.** Product 318, L112, carries «٥٠ مل» in its live `short_description`. The brief says "size not published". It is 50ml at 17.95.

**What the consumption evidence actually established.** Inside a four-piece routine, using the one dataset that measures cleanser, toner, serum and cream in the *same* women, all of them daily users of three-plus layered steps (Yamaguchi M, Araki D, Kanamori T, et al., *J Toxicological Sciences* 42(6):797-814, 2017, Japan Cosmetic Industry Association, products weighed before and after use):

- **30ml serum: about 97 days** (median 0.31 g/day, N=265)
- **200ml toner: about 147 days** by hand (median 1.36 g/day, N=425), about **98 days** with a cotton pad (the study weighed pad users against hand users and measured a **1.5x** multiplier in both seasons; 29% used pads)
- **200ml cleanser: about 217 days** (median 0.92 g/day, N=163)
- **75ml cream: about 250 days** at that median; about **49 days** at the SCCS regulatory P90 of 1.54 g/day (Hall et al. for COLIPA, 18,057 consumers, five European countries)

So the serum does empty first, but **the gap to the next item is about 7 weeks for a typical user and only about 3.6 weeks for a heavy user** — not the 6.7x the pack sizes imply, because the per-application dose differs by only about 3.5x (0.76 g cleanser, 0.74 g toner, 0.24 g cream, 0.21 g serum). The ratio is dose, not volume.

**The honest threat to the premise**, stated because it is real: under US and French measured cleansing (Loretz et al. 2008, N=295, via US EPA *Exposure Factors Handbook* 2011 Table 17-51: mean 4.06 g per use-day), a 200ml cleanser empties in **34 to 49 days, before the serum**. The ordering flips. A pump is a mechanical brake on this; a hand-scoop of gel is not.

**And the sunscreen, which is the point.** 50ml at the weighed clinical mean of 2.01 g/day is **25 days** (Gomez-Berrada et al., 75 clinical safety studies, N=925 person-days, reproduced as SCCS *Notes of Guidance* 12th revision Table 11). At the free-living Japanese daily-wear median of 0.57 g/day it is **88 days** (Yamaguchi 2017, N=288, surveyed in summer). Under-application is the norm, not the exception: a 2024 review of 38 published application values from 1985 to 2020 found **36 of 38 below** the 2 mg/cm² at which SPF is tested, and the only regional signal that exists (a Saudi cross-sectional KAP study: **53.6% apply less than a quarter teaspoon**) puts Jordanian women at the low-dose end. **Planning band: 6 to 12 weeks, and the store has no way to know which.**

**Three corrections that would have broken a naive build:**

1. **"The serum" is five clocks, not one.** Vitamin C is morning-only (~20 weeks). L102 and L104 niacinamide are twice daily (~12 weeks). L119 arbutin is 14 to 20. **L106 سيروم التقشير is 10% + 2% AHA+BHA at two to three times a week: 48 to 71 weeks.** A single serum timer would be wrong by nine months on that SKU and would implicitly invite daily use of a 10% acid. Same split on the toners (L114 hydrating twice daily vs L105 AHA exfoliant) and the cleansers.
2. **For L106 and L103 the bottle expires before it empties.** Period-after-opening arrives first. Any "you must be running low" message there is a false claim she can check in three seconds by picking up the bottle.
3. **Folklore, flagged so nobody builds on it.** "Replenishment emails convert at 8 to 15% for beauty" appears verbatim across at least six agency blogs with no dataset; the only benchmark with a disclosed method (BS&Co, 14 brands, trailing 365 days, Klaviyo last-touch) reports a **0.46% median** entry-email conversion, sixth of seven flows. Also folklore: "cleanser 30 days, serum 45, SPF 60" (implies 6.7 g/day of cleanser, ~7x the measured median) and "300 drops in a 30ml bottle" (standard droppers deliver ~0.05 ml, so closer to 600).

**One sentence:** the fastest clock in a four-piece routine is about 14 weeks and the second item is about 7 weeks behind it, so a routine cannot honestly be resold as a unit for most of a year — and the fastest clock in the catalogue belongs to a 50ml bottle that three of the five routines do not contain.

## 2. The tension, resolved

**The principle: the repurchase interval is set by the minimum clock in the box, not by the size or price of the box. You therefore shorten it by adding breadth to order one, never by selling a smaller version of order one.**

The tension in the brief is real only under the assumption that a shorter interval requires a smaller unit. It does not. It requires a *faster item*. A 48.00 four-piece routine has an interval of about 97 days. The same box plus a 50ml sunscreen has an interval of 42 to 88 days and is worth 65.95. Breadth shortens the interval **while raising the basket**. Only depth lengthens it.

**Why this specific move cannot cannibalise the routine, arithmetically.** I checked this myself because two of the three attack lenses got it wrong in opposite directions:

```
367 − 204 = 70.70 − 48.00 = 22.70
(23.75 L119 − 19.00 L101) + 17.95 L112 = 4.75 + 17.95 = 22.70   ✓ exact

527 − 205 = 70.70 − 47.00 = 23.70
(23.75 L119 − 18.00 L102) + 17.95 L112 = 5.75 + 17.95 = 23.70   ✓ exact
```

The bundle saving is a flat **13.00 in both directions**: 204's parts are 61.00 and it sells at 48.00; 367's parts are 83.70 and it sells at 70.70. So a four-piece **plus** the sunscreen at 65.95 is **not a cheaper clone of the 70.70 five-piece**. It is the same ladder with a cheaper serum, and the serum upgrade is priced at exactly its list difference. There is no arbitrage to teach her, no margin to spend, and no owner approval needed. The owner already built this rung; the move only makes it visible one step earlier.

**The second-order reason this beats every email plan considered.** Under cash on delivery the unit of cost is the **dispatch**, not the order. The store's own feasibility file (`_خطة\جرد-أصول-العلامة.md`) records average unit price 15.33, contribution 2.13 per unit, **13.9% margin**, and a written operational target of **2.3 to 2.7 items per order** with the conclusion that a one-item order barely profits. Every design that shortens the interval by manufacturing a second one-item order is buying a courier leg to sell about 2.50 of contribution. Attaching in order one costs **zero extra legs and zero extra refusal exposure**.

**The residual tension I will not paper over.** Attaching raises cash at the door from 48.00 to 65.95, and Ryan's own written ruling of 6 September (in `library/packages.php`, the block above `LUVIT_UP_ON`) says anything that raises the door number raises refusal risk. That ruling is correct and it is *about a different moment*. Read it precisely: «هي سكّرت حساب ٦٥٫٩٥ لمّا أكّدت. تحويله لـ٧٨٫٥٥ مش «إضافة صنف» · هو **تغيير رقم متّفق عليه بعد الاتفاق**». The objection is to changing an agreed number **after** agreement (mental accounting, peak-end, reactance). Before she confirms, she is still choosing, and the same ruling names the correct placement in Ryan's own words: «**الأصحّ إنها تطلع بصفحة الدفع نفسها**». So: at checkout, unticked, never after confirm. `LUVIT_UP_ON` stays `false`.

### MOVE 1 · «الخطوة الخامسة» — deterministic sunscreen tick at checkout, at list price
**SHIP THIS WEEK · 2 to 4 hours · zero margin spent · no new infrastructure**

**Trigger.** Cart contains a product for which `luvit_pk_ship_free()` is true (its `short_description` parses to 4 or more parts, `packages.php:92`), **and** L112 (id 318) is neither among that bundle's parsed parts nor already a separate cart line. That selects exactly 203, 204, 205 and suppresses 367/527 automatically. Read it through `luvit_pk_parts()` / the cached map — **never hardcode 203/204/205**, which is the rule the shipping snippet already states in red, because a sixth routine breaks a hardcoded list silently.

**Timing and placement.** Cart and checkout page only. **Not** on the routine product page: "your four-piece is incomplete" told before purchase reduces routine conversion to win a second order that has not been earned. **Not** on the thank-you page.

**Channel.** The bump already exists and already works. `packages.php:809-1000` registers a Store API update callback (`woocommerce_store_api_register_update_callback`, namespace `luvit-bump`), which is the only correct path because the checkout is a WooCommerce Block and classic PHP cart hooks are dead there. Today its `best()` function picks a candidate by bundle co-occurrence with a **lower-price tie-break**, which *probably* already lands on L112 for a routine cart but is not guaranteed. **Add one deterministic override to `best()`**: if the cart holds a 4+ piece package without L112, return L112 with fixed reason copy.

**Two implementation notes that will bite otherwise.**
1. **Attach at LIST price, not at 5%.** `luvit_pk_line_pct()` applies `max(tier, LUVIT_PK_BUMP_PCT)` whenever the line carries the `luvit_bump` flag. So add the line with a **different** marker (`luvit_fifth`) that `luvit_pk_line_pct()` ignores. Two reasons for list: 5% of 17.95 is 0.90 JOD, which is the low-benefit band where a premium outperforms a discount (Palazon & Delgado-Ballester, *Psychology & Marketing* 2009), and list price is what lets the copy claim pharmacy parity, which is the trust asset this store actually owns.
2. **The free-shipping claim in the copy is true and I verified it.** The rule in `library/snippets/free-shipping-packages.php` is **order-level**, not per-item, precisely so that "routine + a single" does not become "0 + 2.50". The file says so explicitly.

**Copy.** Common heading and close, one reason line keyed to the routine. Prices render from the existing payload, never typed by hand; wrap `SPF50+‎` in `<span dir="ltr">` or the plus sign flips at the end of the Arabic line.

**On the pharmacy-parity line.** If the owner confirms L112 sells at 17.95 in the pharmacies, add «نفس السعر اللي بالصيدلية» after the price. If he does not confirm it, ship without that line. Do not assert parity on his behalf.

**On the FDA.** Do **not** cite the FDA here, and this is not squeamishness. The actual recommended Sunburn Alert reads "use a sunscreen, **wear protective clothing, and limit sun exposure** while using this product and for a week afterwards." The guidance is AHA-only, is explicitly non-binding, and **explicitly does not apply to products labelled to contain a sunscreen** — which is the completed basket you are selling. Quoting a regulator's warning while deleting the two remedies that generate no revenue, in the surface that charges 17.95 for the third, is one screenshot away from destroying the store's credibility. The 204 line above states the plain pharmacological fact in the store's own voice and names nobody.

**Separately and before launch:** the site currently carries **no sun-sensitivity notice anywhere** — I grepped `library/` and found none on L105, on L106, or on 204. That is a disclosure gap that exists whether or not this move ships, and it belongs on those three product pages with **no CTA anywhere near it**. It is regulatory-adjacent wording, so it goes to the owner (§5).

**Risk.** This raises the first COD basket by up to 37%. It is unticked by default, it is a single 17.95 line on an order already above 47, and the total she sees at «أكّدي الطلب» is the total she agreed to. Move 3 is what tells you within a month whether it raised refusals.

**SHIP THIS WEEK · 1 to 2 hours · zero new sends · zero print cost · zero margin**

### MOVE 3 · Make refusal countable, and log the one number
**SHIP THIS WEEK · half a day · this is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have**

**A correction first, because two of the three review lenses asserted the opposite.** Refusals are **not** invisible. `library/luvit-delivery.php` contains no `set_status` (I checked: zero hits), but the projection sweep in `woo.php` maps `refused → cancelled` and calls `update_status()` at line **1844**, with a guard that will not reverse a terminal status. The real problem is finer: refusals land in `wc-cancelled` **mixed in with every ordinary cancellation**, so you can count cancellations and you cannot count refusals.

**What to build.** At the projection point, write two order meta keys: the terminal stage (`delivered` / `refused`) and, on delivered, the timestamp. Then one small admin CSV export with seven columns: order id, date placed, date delivered, terminal stage, order total, distinct product count, fifth-step tick taken yes/no, contained-a-package yes/no. Nothing else, no dashboard.

Without this, Move 1's kill switch does not exist, and no claim in this document can ever be checked.

### MOVE 4 · One replenishment email, two SKUs, with a postpone button
**MONTH TWO · about a day · do not ship this in launch week**

**Why it waits.** Every date rests on the JCIA weighed medians, which are the lowest-consumption figures in the world for face cream (0.41 g/day mean against 2.05 in the US), applied to Jordanian women for whom **no weighed cosmetic consumption study exists in any Arab country**. The bands are 3x to 8x wide. And under COD a mistimed reminder does not cost a wasted email; it manufactures a half-wanted order that gets refused at the door and the store pays two legs. **Err late, always.** Launch week has no spare attention for a flow whose dates are borrowed from Tokyo.

**Trigger.** `woocommerce_order_status_completed` writes `_luvit_slot_date`; a daily sweep hung on the existing `luvit_d_digest_cron` sends what is due. Use a date-range sweep, **not** `wp_schedule_single_event`, because a single event that misses its window on a low-traffic site never fires again. Suppress `refused` / `cancelled` / `refunded`. Idempotency through the existing `luvit_d_claim_notice()`. Send through `WC()->mailer()` so it inherits the RTL wrapper and Resend.

**Two rows in the table, not twelve.**
- **L112 sunscreen: day 60.** The band is 25 days (clinical, prompted twice-daily) to 88 days (free-living median), and the regional evidence puts Jordanian use at the low-dose end. Day 60 sits in the slow half deliberately.
- **L102 and L104 (twice-daily niacinamide): day 60 if bought alone or in a duo, day 85 if bought inside a routine.** The split is not arbitrary: a bottle inside a bundle carries no visible price of its own and is consumed *less* urgently (Soman & Gourville, "Transaction Decoupling", *JMR* 2001), so the routine window must be **longer**, not the same. Most stores get this backwards.

**Nothing else gets a slot.** Explicitly **no slot for L106 or L103**: both hit period-after-opening long before empty, and «قرب يخلص» on a bottle she can see is half full is a lie she can check in three seconds.

## 4. What I would NOT do

**1. A day-12 or day-16 "fifth step" email to routine buyers.** This was the strongest-sounding idea produced anywhere in this exercise and it dies three separate deaths. It buys a second courier leg to sell one 17.95 item worth about 2.50 in contribution at the store's own 13.9% margin, against a written target of 2.3 to 2.7 items per order. Its audience is, by definition, the women who already declined that exact bottle at checkout, so it re-asks at 17.95 **plus 2.50 shipping** after they saw it free-shipped inside their routine. And the same argument works at checkout at zero marginal dispatch cost. **Do the free attempt before the expensive one.** If checkout take-up turns out to be near zero after 50 orders, revisit it then, with data.

**2. Any post-purchase add-to-order upsell.** Decided by Ryan on 6 September, the reasoning is written into `packages.php`, and `LUVIT_UP_ON` is deliberately `false` with the code left complete. I went looking for a reason to reopen it and did not find one.

**3. A "buy 3 different singles, get 10%" breadth discount.** It is the move that most directly breaks the brand's own rule (`free-shipping-packages.php` header: «استراتيجية العلامة بتمنع الخصومات الأونلاين عشان ما تضرب أسعار الصيدليات»), and on the proposed hook (`woocommerce_cart_calculate_fees`) it would sit **outside** `luvit_pk_line_pct()` and stack additively with the tier and the affiliate coupon. `packages.php:407` already documents that exact failure in red: «نسبة واحدة للسطر، مش خصمين فوق بعض ... الجمع بيوصل ١٥٪ على منتج مفرد وهاد أعلى من الثنائي، فبينقلب السلّم». Do not add a third discount layer six days before launch.

**4. Extending free shipping to any four loose pieces.** It reverses a decision dated 5 September in the same file, and under a raw piece count **two duos would qualify** — two 200ml cleansers and two 200ml toners, the slowest possible basket, nothing under three months. You would pay 2.50 to subsidise the worst order the store can take.

**5. A "time to reorder your routine" email inside six months.** The toner runs about 147 days by hand, the cleansers 105 to 310. She can see the bottles.

**6. A twelve-row consumption timer at launch.** Wrong by nine months on L106, in a direction that implicitly invites daily use of a 10% AHA.

**7. Any three-pack of sunscreen or fast serum.** Three 50ml bottles is 75 to 263 days depending on dose; the tier pays about 5.37 JOD to multiply the store's single best repeat clock by three. **But do not implement this by deleting the product-page card.** `luvit_pk_tiers()` (`packages.php:370`) is one global table that the file itself says feeds both the card and the cart line, so deleting the card leaves 10% live for anyone who types quantity 3 — the page-says-one-thing-cart-charges-another bug this repo has already shipped once. Make it a per-product rule inside `luvit_pk_tier_pct()`, and verify in a live cart at quantity 3, not by reading the page.

**8. The before-and-after photo ritual.** It generates no revenue, creates a personal-data liability the store has no policy for, and asks a woman in Jordan to send a bare-faced photo of herself to a shop that already holds her address and phone number. Cut both ends of it, not just the request.

**9. Quoting the FDA anywhere a purchase is available.** Detailed in Move 1. The edited quote is the single most dangerous line anyone drafted in this exercise.

**10. A fabricated statistic in warm copy.** "Most women stop in week three" was proposed as a disarming opener. The store has six test orders. Replace that whole class with what is actually true: «إحنا لسا جداد، وبدنا نتعلّم من تجربتك».

**11. Promising the owner a measured lift.** Nothing here is measurable for months.

## 5. Decisions that belong to the brand owner

Ryan should carry these to him as decisions, not as recommendations.

1. **The sun-sensitivity notice on L105, L106 and routine 204.** Regulatory-adjacent wording, and the project already has a written rule that this class goes to the site owner. It should ship as a plain disclosure with no CTA near it, and it should exist on the product page **before** it exists in any cart.
2. **The pharmacy-parity claim on L112.** If he confirms 17.95 is the pharmacy price, the copy line ships. If not, it does not.
3. **Removing the 3-unit tier on the sunscreen and the fast serums.** It is a live discount surface being taken away, which is visible.
4. **The flat 13.00 bundle saving.** It makes the 70.70 five-piece a **15.53%** saving while the 48.00 four-piece is **21.31%**, so the top of the ladder shows the worst rate and the «وفّرتِ» line will display the identical 13.00 on both. Flagging it, not fixing it.
5. **Whether the 5% and 10% quantity tiers on singles are compatible with the pharmacy-parity rule at all**, since 10% off a single *is* a single sold below pharmacy price. That contradiction predates every idea in this document.
6. **The loyalty programme's counting basis.** `luvit_loyalty_state()` (`woo.php:1302`) counts **orders**, not value, and is gated behind `is_user_logged_in()`. So a woman who splits into small orders reaches the gift faster than the flagship routine buyer, and guests — most COD customers — accrue nothing at all. That is the same inversion that killed "every 5th order ships free", running in the other direction.
7. **Anything that spends margin:** a free-shipping token on a post-routine single, an in-box sachet, a printed card, and what the tenth-order gift actually is.
8. **The phone call that is worth more than all of the above.** The courier's real outbound cost, return-leg cost, and whether there is a percentage COD collection fee. The 2.50 in the Jordan zone is recorded in `_خطة\حالة-المشروع.md` as **«توصيل موحّد 2.50 وهمي»** — a placeholder. Every economic claim in this document, mine included, is conditional on that number. It is an hour, not a build.

## 6. What to measure from day one

**The single number that proves or kills this plan:**

> **Days between the `delivered` stage on order N and the placement of order N+1, split by whether the fifth-step tick was taken on order N.**

If routine buyers who attached the sunscreen do not reorder measurably sooner than routine buyers who did not, the whole thesis is wrong and should be killed rather than defended. That is the entire bet, stated so it can lose.

**Four supporting fields, all free once Move 3 exists:**

| what | why it matters | decision threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Attach take-rate at checkout | tells you whether Move 1 is doing anything at all before you argue about intervals | 50 routine orders |
| **Refusal rate split by whether the attach was taken** | the fast kill switch. If attaching raises refusals, switch it off before anyone blames the copy or the courier | 50 delivered orders |
| Items per order, against the written 2.3 to 2.7 target | the store's own profitability condition | continuous |
| Postpone-click distribution per SKU (Move 4) | the store's first real Jordanian consumption data, replacing Tokyo's medians with Amman's | 50 clicks per SKU |

At roughly 50 repeat orders, every date in this document should be reset from LUV IT's own reorder gaps and the Yamaguchi medians retired. Until then, every timing number here is a hypothesis derived from weighed studies of women who do not live in Jordan, and it should be described that way to the owner before he expects a number.
